MoE router metrics, explained

A friendly guide to the metrics that describe where the router sends tokens. These are routing/load-health signals—not proof that an expert’s output improves the model.

The rule of thumb: Router metrics answer “is work being distributed sensibly?” Viability metrics answer “is that work learning and useful?” A perfectly balanced router can still feed a functionally silent expert layer.
Load distribution

Dispatched expert-load entropy

Imagine sharing cookies among experts. High entropy means the cookies are spread fairly evenly; low entropy means a few experts get nearly all of them.

Technical view

Normalized entropy of each layer’s post-capacity token distribution. 1.0 is uniform; 0.0 means one expert receives everything.
Healthy: stable and reasonably high for the intended routing policy.
Watch: a sudden fall, especially with rising zero-load experts.
No work assigned

Expert dead count

This counts experts that got no dispatched tokens at all during the logging window—like workers who received no tasks.

Technical view

Number of experts whose post-capacity dispatched assignment count is zero. It reflects actual work after dropping/capacity effects, not just the router’s initial preference.
Healthy: zero or an expected small value for sparse/top-k routing.
Watch: persistent growth or the same experts repeatedly at zero.
Before capacity limits

Selected zero-load expert slots

Before any capacity limit can discard work, did the router choose this expert even once? If not, it was ignored from the start.

Technical view

Counts layer/expert pairs with zero selected tokens. This distinguishes routing avoidance from post-selection dropping.
Healthy: low, or consistent with a deliberately sparse policy.
Watch: recurring zeros for particular experts: a router specialization or starvation signal.
Underused work

Selected near-dead expert slots

An expert may not be completely ignored, but it might get only crumbs while its peers get meals.

Technical view

Counts selected loads below 10% of the mean selected load in an active layer. It is a relative imbalance detector, not a statement about output quality.
Healthy: low and non-persistent.
Watch: many slots or a long-lived group of underused experts.
Long-term starvation

Persistent selected near-dead slots

One quiet shift is normal. A worker getting crumbs for one hundred shifts is a pattern.

Technical view

Near-dead layer/expert slots whose streak reaches 100 logging windows. This turns noisy one-window imbalance into a persistence canary.
Healthy: zero, or investigated when nonzero.
Watch: any persistent cohort; compare with its weights, gradients, and routed output.
Worst imbalance

Selected expert max violation

This asks: “How much more work did the busiest expert get than a fair share?” It highlights the worst hog.

Technical view

The maximum deviation of selected expert load from the layer’s uniform target. It is useful as a worst-case canary alongside average entropy.
Healthy: stable and modest for your top-k/group routing design.
Watch: sharp spikes or a drift upward over training.
Capacity effects

Dropped assignment fraction

The router may choose an expert, but a full inbox can force some of those assignments to be thrown away.

Technical view

1 − dispatched assignments / selected assignments. It separates bad routing preference from capacity/token-dropping pressure.
Healthy: near zero for dropless configurations.
Watch: high or rising values; capacity may be hiding selected-load health.
Confidence

Router mean maximum probability

For every token, the router has favourite experts. This is the average strength of that favourite.

Technical view

Mean per-token maximum score from the configured router score distribution. It measures concentration/confidence, not whether a choice is correct.
Healthy: stable relative to your score function and top-k setting.
Watch: abrupt saturation or collapse; compare with score entropy and load balance.
Choice uncertainty

Router mean score entropy

Does the router see one clear winner, or several plausible experts for each token?

Technical view

Mean normalized entropy of the configured per-token score distribution. Higher entropy means choices are more diffuse; lower entropy means more concentrated.
Healthy: a stable regime consistent with your routing strategy.
Watch: rapid changes, especially alongside logit-spread or load changes.
Router preference spread

Expert logit spread

This measures how differently the router scores the experts overall. A wide spread means it strongly prefers some experts.

Technical view

Population standard deviation across per-expert mean router logits. It is a bias/preference canary, not a probability or a load count.
Healthy: stable, with interpretation based on the score function and model phase.
Watch: a growing spread paired with starvation or falling load entropy.
How to act

Suggested reading order

First inspect dead / near-dead / persistent slots and entropy. Next use dropped assignment fraction to decide whether capacity is the culprit. Then read mean maximum probability, score entropy, and logit spread together to understand router confidence and preference. Finally, use the viability metrics below to determine whether routing health translates to useful computation.

Do not conclude that balanced load means healthy experts. It only means the router distributed assignments.

Expert viability & causal-impact metrics

These metrics are opt-in. The parameter, gradient, and routed-output metrics are emitted by the current diagnostics; update-decomposition and paired-mask values are shown here as the intended next diagnostic layer.

Expert size

Expert weight RMS — median, p10, minimum

RMS is the typical size of an expert’s weights. The median describes a normal expert; p10 and minimum reveal the weak tail.

Technical view

Combined root-mean-square across each routed expert’s trainable matrices, summarized per layer as expert_weight_rms_median, p10, and min.
Healthy: a stable distribution without an isolated collapsing tail.
Watch: p10/min falling far below the median or drifting sharply downward.
Movement from initialization

Weight RMS relative to initialization

Did the expert actually change from its starting point, or is it still almost the same machine it was on day one?

Technical view

Current combined RMS divided by the captured initialization RMS, reported as expert_weight_rms_relative_to_init_median per layer.
Healthy: interpret as a stable training trend, compared with peer layers.
Watch: values collapsing toward zero or a single layer diverging from the rest.
Collapse canary

Collapsed-expert fraction

This is the share of experts whose weights have shrunk to less than one tenth of their original typical size.

Technical view

expert_weight_collapsed_frac counts experts with current RMS below 10% of initialization RMS. The 10% threshold is an internal diagnostic definition, not a tuning knob.
Healthy: zero, or a small stable value with an understood cause.
Watch: an increasing fraction, especially alongside low load and gradients.
Learning signal

Expert gradient RMS

Gradients are the training instructions. A tiny gradient means an expert is being given very little direction for how to improve.

Technical view

Combined RMS of finalized per-expert gradients, reported as expert_grad_rms_median per layer. It measures update signal magnitude, not whether an update is beneficial.
Healthy: a nonzero, stable scale relative to similar layers and training phase.
Watch: a persistently weak tail paired with static weights or tiny routed output.
Routed path only

Routed-expert output RMS

How loud is the output of the routed experts themselves? Shared-expert output is deliberately left out.

Technical view

routed_expert_output_rms is measured before shared-expert addition, from squared-sum/count statistics without retaining activations.
Healthy: non-trivial and stable compared with similar layers.
Watch: near zero while routing/load metrics look healthy.
Relative contribution

Routed-output RMS ratios

Is the routed-expert signal a meaningful voice in the layer, or a whisper next to the input and final output?

Technical view

routed_expert_output_to_input_rms divides routed RMS by MoE-input RMS. ...to_layer_output_rms divides it by final MoE-output RMS. They are relative diagnostics, not universal pass/fail numbers.
Healthy: stable, non-trivial ratios relative to peer layers.
Watch: ratios near zero or a sudden layer-specific collapse.
Optimizer decomposition · planned

Adaptive update, decay update & update/weight ratio

Weights can receive gradients yet barely move. These metrics separate the optimizer’s learning step from its shrinkage step.

Technical view

For Adam/AdamW-compatible state, planned metrics are expert_adaptive_update_rms, expert_decay_update_rms, and expert_update_to_weight_ratio. Unsupported optimizers should omit them.
Healthy: a stable ratio and sensible adaptive/decay balance for the training regime.
Watch: updates vanishing versus weights, or decay dominating adaptive updates.
Causal validation · planned

Masked-layer NLL / PPL delta

Temporarily mute one layer’s routed experts. If validation gets worse, they were helping; if nothing changes, they may be doing little.

Technical view

A paired probe evaluates the same cached validation batches normally and with only one physical layer’s routed output zeroed. It logs token-weighted masked_layer_nll_delta and masked_layer_ppl_delta.
Healthy: a clearly positive NLL delta, allowing for normal numerical noise.
Watch: near-zero delta with apparently healthy routing; this is direct functional-silence evidence.
